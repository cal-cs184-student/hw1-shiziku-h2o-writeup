Overview

In this assignment I built a software triangle rasterizer that turns SVG drawings into pixels. I implemented triangle traversal with a bounding-box + point-in-triangle test, grid-based supersampling for antialiasing, 2D affine transforms, barycentric color interpolation, and texture mapping with nearest/bilinear pixel sampling and mipmap level sampling. The most interesting parts for me were seeing why every sampling decision is a tradeoff: supersampling and bilinear filtering both improve quality but cost memory/time, while mipmaps specifically solve minification aliasing in a very elegant, screen-space derivative based way.

Task 1: Triangle Rasterization

A triangle is rasterized by first computing its bounding box in pixel coordinates, then testing every sub-pixel sample inside that box against the triangle. For each candidate sample I compute barycentric coordinates (alpha, beta, gamma); the sample is inside the triangle when all three coordinates are non-negative. Boundary samples are kept, and the computation is independent of vertex winding order. Because I only iterate over the bounding box (not the whole framebuffer), the cost is proportional to the triangle's screen area rather than the full image; another standard approach is per-edge line equations with a consistent sign test, which has the same asymptotic cost. Points and lines are filled by fill_pixel.

Task 2: Supersampling Antialiasing

Supersampling renders the scene at a higher resolution and downsamples it to the output resolution. I keep a sample_buffer of size width * height * sample_rate, where each output pixel owns a sqrt(sample_rate) x sqrt(sample_rate) grid of sub-samples. Triangles fill individual sub-samples, while points and lines fill every sub-sample of their pixel with the same color. After all primitives are rasterized, resolve_to_framebuffer averages each pixel's sub-samples and converts the floating color to 8-bit/channel frame-buffer values. At rate 1 edges are hard aliased; at 4 and 16 the partial-coverage pixels blend toward the background, producing visibly smoother triangle edges.

Task 4: Barycentric Coordinates / Color Interpolation

Barycentric coordinates express any point inside a triangle as a weighted average of its three vertices, p = alpha*p0 + beta*p1 + gamma*p2 with alpha+beta+gamma=1. Each weight is the (signed) area of the opposite sub-triangle divided by the total triangle area, so the weights are naturally interpolated across the surface. For vertex colors I compute the same barycentric weights at each sample and blend the colors: c = alpha*c0 + beta*c1 + gamma*c2. The color wheel below is basic/test7.svg at sample rate 1.

Task 5: Pixel Sampling / Texture Mapping

Pixel sampling maps a continuous UV coordinate inside a triangle to a color in the texture. Nearest-neighbor sampling picks the single nearest texel (floor(uv * size)), so it is fast but blocky under magnification and noisy under minification. Bilinear sampling takes the four surrounding texels around a shifted sample position and linearly blends them, giving smoother filtering with only slightly more work. The four clips compare nearest vs. bilinear at 1 and 16 samples per pixel on svg/texmap/test1.svg.

Task 6: Level Sampling

Level sampling (mipmapping) pre-filters the texture into a pyramid of progressively smaller images; at render time we choose a level whose texel density matches the screen pixel density. My get_level computes texture-coordinate differences between the current sample and samples one pixel to the right and above, scales those differences into texel space, then returns log2(max(|dudx, dvdx|, |dudy, dvdy|)). sample then dispatches to L_ZERO (always level 0), L_NEAREST (round to the closest level), or L_LINEAR (blend the two surrounding levels; with P_LINEAR this is trilinear filtering).

Tradeoffs: pixel sampling is cheapest but cannot fight minification aliasing; level sampling adds ~1/3 memory for mipmaps plus extra texel reads but removes minification shimmer; supersampling is the most general (it also antialiases geometry) but grows memory and time linearly with the sample count.

These four use my own generated texture 123.png (a fine concentric-ring pattern) mapped through task6_zoneplate.svg. The far/minified regions of the snail-like grid alias badly with L_ZERO; L_NEAREST picks a coarser mip level and largely removes that moire, and bilinear pixel sampling smooths the remaining detail.
